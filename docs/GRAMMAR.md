Introductions explain patterns and gloss the words used. Practice includes choosing
whole sentences, interpreting meaning, filling a particle/ending and identifying
the topic/owner. These are multiple-choice exercises, not free typing or draggable
sentence construction. Kana-only sentence spelling keeps kanji recall out of the task.

Conventions:
- は is a topic particle pronounced wa; it is not presented as a translation of “is”.
- です and its negatives are taught in noun statements, not as universal verb rules.
- じゃないです is a conversational polite negative. ではありません is also valid;
  equivalent negatives are not competing choices.
- Ownership is the focus for の, not its only use.
- Demonstrative questions specify speaker/listener distance. Other uses are outside scope.
- Topic omission is explained. An explicit topic is retained for most practice.
- Sentence-final か is taught for these polite questions, not as a requirement for all questions.
- “My friend” translations use the context stated in the lesson.
- This is a first grammar milestone, not a complete N5 curriculum.

Progress uses new grammar-* IDs. Existing question sets, best scores, saved words,
flashcards and the Android bootstrap workaround are unchanged.
Mistake review uses the shared lesson engine and does not replace the original score.

## Action-sentence extension
Six familiar verbs are introduced as explicit dictionary/polite pairs: たべる →
たべます, のむ → のみます, よむ → よみます, みる → みます, いく →
いきます, くる → きます. Full verb-group conjugation rules are deferred.
Non-past meaning depends on context; one exercise explicitly supplies tomorrow.
ません negates these polite verbs; noun negatives from earlier lessons are not
presented as interchangeable verb endings.

Object exercises explicitly ask for the direct-object marker, so a possible
contrastive は reading is not treated as an ungrammatical alternative.
を is taught with its ordinary modern o pronunciation.
で is limited to action locations here, not destinations or existence.
Context identifies the subject where an English translation supplies I or you.

## Destinations, times and past actions
に and へ are both accepted for the taught movement examples; they are never
competing answers to an unrestricted destination blank. Directional へ is pronounced
e. Action-location で is contrasted with destination に.

Time practice explicitly teaches さんじ, はちじ and くじ. に marks clock times in
the selected pattern; あした and きのう normally appear without に in simple sentences.
The course does not claim that every time expression requires に.

Past practice changes the already-taught polite verb forms to ました and
ませんでした. It includes questions, replies, and irregular きます → きました.
Full verb-group conjugation rules remain outside this update.

## Noun past and adjectives
Past noun statements use でした and conversational polite じゃなかったです.
ではありませんでした is recognised as another valid past negative, never used as
a competing wrong answer to an unrestricted negative question.

Four regular い-adjectives introduce direct noun modification and くないです.
な-adjectives introduce な before nouns, without な before です. きれい is explicitly
identified as a な-adjective despite its final い sound. The irregular negative of
いい and adjective linking are deferred.
Introductions gloss new words; these grammar-only glosses do not expand the 48-word
vocabulary bank. All questions have one intended answer in the stated context.

## Existence and locations
あります is used for things and normally plants; います for people and animals in
these examples. Avoid the misleading claim that all living things use います.
Place + に + noun + が introduces what is present; noun + は + place + に locates
a known topic. A question contrasting these patterns explicitly requests the topic,
rather than labelling the other grammatical sentence incorrect.

の links a reference object with うえ, した or なか. うえ can mean on or above;
desk examples explicitly use on. Location に is distinguished from action-location
で. Japanese nouns do not necessarily specify number; singular English examples
are contextual translations. Counters and comprehensive は/が rules are deferred.

## Past descriptions and mixed review
Regular い-adjectives replace final い with かったです or くなかったです.
な-adjectives use でした or じゃなかったです, including きれい. Past examples
supply yesterday or at-that-time context. Other valid polite negatives are not
used as competing incorrect answers.

The eight-question mixed review reuses taught vocabulary and patterns. It is a
normal replayable lesson with its own saved score, not a mastery certification,
placement test or prerequisite gate. It does not change earlier lesson scores.

## Preferences and invitations
すき is a な-adjective, not a ます verb. が introduces the thing liked in the taught
pattern; は commonly marks it as a topic in negative examples, without claiming
that が is always incorrect in negatives. あまり + negative means not very much
for these preferences, and does not imply that the speaker never eats a food.

Invitation questions explicitly establish the shared-action context for ませんか.
ましょう suggests a shared action or accepts an invitation here; other uses are
outside this lesson. The unfinished あしたはちょっと… is taught as a contextual
polite refusal, not a universal translation of ちょっと. These lessons follow the
existing mixed checkpoint, which continues to review only the earlier patterns.

## Wants: things and actions
ほしい expresses wanting a thing; たい follows a verb’s ます stem to express wanting
to act. Their negatives follow the い-adjective pattern. The course recognises
ほしくありません and たくありません without making valid alternatives compete.
Both を and が can mark the object in the taught water/drinking example; the final
question explicitly accepts both rather than overgeneralising one particle rule.
Statements concern the speaker’s wishes; questions concern a friend’s wishes in
gift/planning contexts. They are not taught as universal polite requests or
invitations, or as a way to assert a third person’s private wishes.
